# Knowledge Universe — a quick guide for testers

Thanks for trying this out. This is an early prototype, and your job is less to
"use it correctly" than to **poke at it and tell me where it breaks.**

## What this is

Most experiments that don't work never get published. That absent knowledge —
the interaction you couldn't detect, the compound with no effect, the assay that
went nowhere — gets quietly re-generated by lab after lab. This is an attempt to
give those negative and inconclusive results a home: a shared, searchable,
*citable* record of what's already been tried and found wanting, so the next
person doesn't repeat a dead end blind.

What you're testing today is the **intake** part: a conversational agent that
turns your messy notes into a structured entry. You talk (or paste notes), it
extracts the useful fields, asks about the things that matter, and shows you
exactly what it recorded before anything is saved.

## How to use it

1. Open the link I sent you and enter the shared password.
2. Describe a negative or inconclusive result from your own work — a real one.
   You can paste raw lab notes, or just type it out casually. Messy is fine;
   messy is actually better for testing.
3. The agent will ask you a few pointed questions — usually about things like
   whether you ran a positive control, whether the result was about *binding* vs.
   an *effect*, how many replicates, and which conditions matter. Answer honestly,
   including "I don't know" or "I didn't record that" where true.
4. When it says it's ready, click **Review & confirm**. Read this carefully — see
   below.
5. If it looks right, click **Submit entry**. If it doesn't, that's the most
   useful outcome of all — tell me what was wrong.

Nothing is saved until you hit Submit. Take as long as you like.

## The one thing I most want you to scrutinize

This whole system is only worth building if the entries are **honest**. A
negative result that's quietly embellished — a control that's implied but wasn't
run, conditions the tool guessed at — is worse than no entry at all.

So the tool is built to **extract only what you actually say, and to mark
everything else as "unknown" rather than guess.** The confirmation screen splits
what it recorded into three parts:

- **STATED** — things it took directly from your words
- **INFERRED** — things it read between the lines (it should show its reasoning)
- **STILL UNKNOWN** — things you didn't mention, left honestly blank

**Please read the INFERRED and UNKNOWN sections closely.** The questions I care
about most:

- Did it put anything in STATED that you didn't actually say?
- Did it INFER something it should have just asked about, or left unknown?
- Did it ever quietly "helpfully" fill in a detail — a buffer, a control, a
  number — that you never gave it?
- Did it try to write your *confidence level* or *caveats* for you? (It's not
  supposed to — those are meant to be your judgment.)

If you can make it record something you didn't say, that's the single most
valuable bug you can find. Try to trip it up.

## A good way to test it

Rather than a clean, textbook result, try one where the details were **messy or
incomplete** — a pilot with few replicates, an experiment where you're not sure
the assay was sensitive enough, a result with conditions you'd have to dig up
your notes to reconstruct. The gaps are exactly where the tool's honesty gets
tested. A tidy, complete result won't reveal much.

## What to send back

Whatever you noticed — informally is fine. Especially:

- Anything it recorded that you didn't say, or got subtly wrong
- Questions it asked that felt useless, or important questions it *failed* to ask
- Points where it felt like a chore vs. points where it felt genuinely easier
  than filling out a form
- Whether the final entry actually captured what makes your result useful (or not)
  to someone who might otherwise repeat it
- Anything confusing, annoying, or broken

## A few honest caveats

- It's a prototype. It may be slow, occasionally lose the thread, or restart on
  you. If a session vanishes, just start again — anything you submitted is safe.
- Your data stays private to this small test group; entries go to a folder only I
  review. Please use real results, but nothing you'd consider sensitive or
  unpublishable-if-leaked — treat it as a trusted-but-informal setting.
- It needs to ask you things a form wouldn't. That's intentional — the questions
  are where the value is — but tell me if it overdoes it.

That's it. Thanks for kicking the tires — the point right now is to find out
what's wrong while it's still cheap to fix.
